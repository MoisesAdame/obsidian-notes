## Definición
$$
{X(t) : t \in T}
$$
where X(t) is a random variable
t is time.

Can have discrete time and continous

Random Variable: X: S -> R. A function that assigns a real value to each outcome

P(E_{i}) = P(E_{i} : T_{i - 1} < T_{i + 1}) / P(E_{i} | T_{i-1} > T_{i+1})

## Preliminaries
### Independence of Random Variables
Independent if $P(X = x,  Y = y) = P(X = x) P(Y = y)$

### Expected Value
$$
E[X] = \sum_{x} xP(X = x)
$$
Two common ways:
1. If X1, X2, ..., Xn, then E[X] = sum_i = 1 E[X_i]
2. E[X] = E(E(E|Y))

Proof of 1
$$
E[X + Y] = \sum_{x}\sum_{y}(x+y)P(X = x, Y = y)
$$

Proof of 2
$$
E[X] = E[E[X | Y]]
$$

$$
E[E[X | Y]] = \sum_{y}E[X | Y = y] P(Y = y) = \sum_{y} \sum_{_{x}} x P(X = x , Y = y) = E[X]
$$

### Example Keys

### Poisson and Binomial DIstribution
#### Poisson
Let X be a bounded non negative random variable:
$$
E[{X \choose i}] \approx \frac{\lambda^i}{i!}
$$

then:
$$
P(X = j) \approx \frac{\lambda^j e^{-\lambda}}{j!}
$$
Proof
$$
P(X = j) = E[I_{j}]
$$
$$
I_{j} = \begin{matrix}
1 \text{ si X = j} \\
0 \text{ otherwise}
\end{matrix}
$$

$$
I_{i} = C_{x}^j (1- 1)^{x - j} = C_{x}^j \sum_{k = 0}^k (-1)^k = \sum_{k = 0}^{\infty} C_{x}^{i+k}C_{j+k}^k (-1)^k
$$
 Entonces
 $$
 P(X = j) = \sum_{k = 0}^{\infty} E[C_{x}^{i+k}]C_{j+k}^k (-1)^k \approx \sum_{k = 0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^{j+k}}{(j+k)!} C_{j+k}^k (-1)^k
 $$
$$
C_{x}^i = \sum_{j = 1}^{C_{n}^i} X_{j}
$$

$$
E[C_{X}^i] = C_{n}^i p^i = \frac{n (n-1) \cdots (c-i+1)}{i!} p^i \approx \frac{(np)^i}{i!}
$$

#### How To Bound the approximation error for general n and p
