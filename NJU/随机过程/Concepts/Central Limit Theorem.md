## Subtitle
Value
=======

The **Central Limit Theorem (CLT)** states that, given a sufficiently large sample size, the sampling distribution of the sample mean will approximate a normal distribution, regardless of the shape of the population distribution from which the samples are drawn.

Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2$. Then, as $n \to \infty$, the standardized sample mean
$$
Z_n = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}
$$
converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, where
$$
\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i
$$
is the sample mean.
