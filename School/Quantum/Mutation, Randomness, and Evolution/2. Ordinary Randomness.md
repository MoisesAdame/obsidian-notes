## 2.1 Introduction
### Common Mutation Classification
Commonly, mutations are classified in two:
1. **Common Mutations:** All single-nucleotide substitutions or point mutations.
2. **Other Mutations:** They are ignored.

This classification is useful if we don't care about **mutation in evolution**.
The problem is that **other mutations** are the most common.

Every mutation emerges through **different mechanisms**.

In [[A. Mutation Exemplars]], we can find four examples of $C \to T$ nucleotide substitution.
