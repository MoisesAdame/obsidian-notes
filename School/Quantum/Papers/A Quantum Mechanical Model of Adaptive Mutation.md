## Abstract
**Whats the problem?**
	Mutations not only occur due to *evolutionary change*, they also occur due to *adaptive mutations*

**What was done?**
	Use a *wave function* (that is a coherent superposition of the proton in the state of shifted and unshifted). Then use *decoherence* to *entangle* the state with the surroundings.
	Estimate decoherence times ([Explained here]([[40.A Quantum Coherence & Decoherence]])) for protons inside DNA.

**What as found?**
	Quantum *coherence is maintained* for biological time-scales. A higher *decoherence rate* increases *mutated states*.

## 1 Introduction
Previously it was thought that mutations were random, but an experiment showed that a lactose rich medium produced more mutations related to lactose fermentation than a normal medium, thus proving the existence of **adaptive/directed mutation**.

Adaptive mutation is different from normal mutation because:
1. Cells do *not divide* or do it rarely.
2. Is *time-dependent*, not replication-dependent.
3. Appear if there is *selective pressure*.

A lot of people commented things like:
1. Mutations are generated by *quantum jumps* in protons of DNA bases.
2. DNA is a *superposition of mutational states*.

For that to be possible, the wave function must remain *coherent enough* for it to *interact with the environment*.

## 2 Model and Results
### 2.1 Initiation of Mutations
The Lowdin's mutation model:
1. Quantum tunneling generates a DNA tautomeric form.
2. When repairing the strand, an incorrect base is added.

The model starts because of an **H-bonded proton** between two adjacent sites, it can be described as a superposition of the tunneled and not tunneled states, follows:
$$
\ket{\phi_{\text{proton}}} = \alpha \ket{\phi_{\text{not tun.}}} + \beta \ket{\phi_{\text{tun.}}}   
$$

Then the correct base (C) and the incorrect base (T) are incorporated to the model in their respective estates of the superposition, that describes the DNA:
$$
\ket{\psi_{G}} = \alpha\ket{\phi_{\text{not tun.}}}\ket{C}  + \beta\ket{\phi_{\text{tun.}}}\ket{T}
$$

After **transcription and translation**, we can have two aminoacidic outcomes, *arginine* (normal protein) and *histidine* (mutant protein), which causes or not the ability to ferment lactose.
$$
\ket{\psi_{\text{cell}}} = \alpha\ket{\phi_{\text{not tun.}}}\ket{C}\ket{\text{Arg}}  + \beta\ket{\phi_{\text{tun.}}}\ket{T}\ket{\text{His}} 
$$

The time in which all this happened is estimated to be **between 1s and 100s**, just enough for **superposition of states** to be maintained in a cell.

## 3 Decoherence
Due to decoherence, initially we have a pure state, but after te *interaction of the environment* with the system, our system becomes **entangled** with the environment.

This is important, because when we do a measurement, the **wave function collapses**, and this collapse is related to the interaction between the system and the environment. For instance, we know that the **density matrix** in its diagonal has the *classical terms* and in its off-diagonal has the *interference terms* (the ones that cause a quantum behavior). Thus, after interaction between the system and the environment, the interference terms cancel out, leading to **quantum decoherence**.

When talking about the cell, we can think of it as a **quantum measuring device**, that checks the processes going inside of it causing, decoherence.

A function that describes decoherence time is the following:
$$
t_{D} = t_{R} \left( \frac{\lambda_{T}}{\Delta x} \right)
$$
Where:
- **Thermal de Broglie Wavelength ($\lambda_{T}$)**: $\lambda_{T}=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{ 2mk_{B}T }}$
	- Gas particle's mass ($m$)
	- Boltzmann constant ($k_{B}$)
	- Temperature ($T$)
- **Distance ($\Delta$):** Distance between protons.
- **Relaxation Time ($t_{R}$):** The time taken for the wave packets to dissipate the energy difference between the coherent state

After plugging in the values we see that: 
$$
t_{D} = 0.29 t_{R}
$$
Unfortunately, we don't know values for $t_{R}$, but research suggests that they are large enough. Which means that **quantum coherence** is maintained for enough time to *allow the cell become a the previously described superposition*.

## 4 Accelerated Decoherence by the Environment for Mutant States
### Non-Adaptive Conditions (No Lactose)
In this case the cell **cannot distinguish** between the two states of $\ket{\psi_{\text{cell}}}$, thus, being similar to a coin toss. At the same time, decoherence time **remains the same**.

### Adaptive Conditions (With Lactose)
The presence of lactose is **part of the environment**, thus the new superposition looks as follows:
$$
\ket{\psi_{\text{cell}}} = \alpha\ket{\phi_{\text{not tun.}}}\ket{C}\ket{\text{Arg}}\ket{\text{lactose}} + \beta\ket{\phi_{\text{tun.}}}\ket{T}\ket{\text{His}}\ket{\text{lactose}}
$$

This effect is rapidly spread across the cell, causing a  **reduction in the decoherence time**, due to the fact that the mass instead of being for one proton, is for many. Finally leading into the classical states:
$$
\ket{\phi_{\text{not tun.}}}\ket{C}\ket{\text{Arg}}\ket{\text{lactose}} ~ \text{or} ~ \ket{\phi_{\text{tun.}}}\ket{T}\ket{\text{His}}\ket{\text{lactose}}
$$

This **lower decoherence time** (with respect to the non-adaptive one), means that because the cell will *decohere faster*, there will be *more mutations*.

## 5 Discussion
Physicists have proposed that biological systems are properly described by **quantum mechanics**. This explains why thinking of DNA as a **superposition of mutation and not mutation states**, is very true.

What is important of living organisms is that the interaction between the cells and the environment, allows us to determine their **macroscopic behavior**, using quantum mechanics.

Decoherence wipes out quantum phenomena going on in the microscopic level. This is related too measurement, which has an influence in the system.

Interaction between the wave function and the environment allows the cell to simultaneously sample the vast mutational spectra as a quantum superposition.
