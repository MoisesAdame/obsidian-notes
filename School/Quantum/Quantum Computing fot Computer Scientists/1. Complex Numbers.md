## 1.1 Basic Definitions
Everything starts with the following equaition

$$
x^{2}+1=0
$$
The solution is
$$
x=\sqrt{ -1 } \not\in \mathbb{R}
$$

We have two types of numbers:
- Imaginary $=\{z | z=ai, a \in \mathbb{R}, i = \sqrt{ -1 }\}$
- Complex $=\{z | z=a + bi, a,b \in \mathbb{R}, i = \sqrt{ -1 }\}$

**Fundamental Theorem of Algebra**: Every polynomial equation of one variable with **complex coefficients** has a **complex solution**.

## 1.2 The Algebra of Complex Numbers
### The Basics
**Complex Number:** Is an ordered pair of real numbers.

$$
z=(a, b)
$$

### Sum & Difference
The sum is both **associative** and **commutative**. Its identity is $0+0i$.
$$
z_{1}+z_{2}=(a_{1}, b_{1})+(a_{2}, b_{2})=(a_{1}+a_{2}, b_{1}+b_{2})
$$
$$
z_{1}-z_{2}=(a_{1}, b_{1})-(a_{2}, b_{2})=(a_{1}-a_{2}, b_{1}-b_{2})
$$

### Multiplication & Division
Multiplication and division are both **associative** and **commutative**. Also, they are **distributive** over the sum. Its identity is $1+0i$.
$$
z_{1}z_{2}=(a_{1}, b_{1})(a_{2}, b_{2})=(a_{1}a_{2}-b_{1}b_{2}, a_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}a_{2})
$$
$$
(x,y)=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}=\frac{(a_{1}, b_{1})}{(a_{2}, b_{2})}
$$
$$
x=\frac{a_{1}a_{2}+b_{1}b_{2}}{a_{2}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}}, y=\frac{b_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}b_{2}}{a_{2}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}}
$$

### Modulus
The "length" of the complex number.
$$
|z|=\sqrt{ a^{2} + b^{2}}
$$
### Conjugation
Changing the sign in the imaginary part. Also $z$ and $\bar{z}$ are **complex conjugates**.
$$
\bar{z}=a-bi
$$

Due to its properties, complex conjugation is a **bijective function** because:
1. Is Injective: B can't have more than one A.
2. Is Surjective: Every B has one A.

## 1.3 The Geometry of Complex Numbers
### Complex Plane
Because complex numbers are an ordered pair of real numbers, they can be seen as vectors that lie on the **complex plane**. Where x are the real numbers and y the imaginary numbers.

![[fig1.2.png|300]]

### Sum & Difference
Sum and difference can be understood using the paralelogram rule.

### Multiplication & Division
To understand it, we have to shift from cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates, where.
$$
(a,b)\to(\rho,\theta)
$$
$$
\rho=\sqrt{ a^{2} + b^{2} }, \theta = \tan ^{-1}\left( \frac{b}{a} \right)
$$
$$
a = \rho \cos(\theta), b = \rho \sin(\theta)
$$

After some algebraic manipulations, we can see that the **multiplication** ends up like:
$$
z_{1}z_{2} = (\rho_{1} \rho_{2}, \theta_{1} + \theta_{2})
$$

And the **division** ends up like:
$$
\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}= \left( \frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}, \theta_{1} - \theta_{2} \right)
$$

### Powers
The powers look like this:
$$
z^{n}=(\rho^{n}, n\theta)
$$
### Important Formulas
#### Cartesian to Polar
$$
z=\rho \left( \cos(\theta) + i\sin(\theta) \right) 
$$

#### Euler's Formula
$$
e^{i\theta} = \cos(\theta) + i\sin(\theta)
$$

#### De Moivre's Formula
$$
\left( e^{i\theta} \right)^{n} = \cos(n\theta) + i\sin(n\theta)
$$
