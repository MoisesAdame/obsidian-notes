## 1.1 Mendelian Laws of Inheritance
### Introduction
**Genetics:** The field of biology concerned with inheritance.

What **Gregor Mendel** was trying to do in the **1860's** was to prove one of the two breeding theories:
1. Blending Inheritance: If a breeds with b, will have an average between a and b.
2. Particulate Inheritance: If a has gene x determinant, and b doesn't, the offspring will have gene x form a.

### Mendel's Experiments
A **character** is a set of **traits**. A character is an observable physical feature.

In his experiments, he bred two different plants, which were the **parental plants (P)**. These plants produced a **First Filial Generation (F1)**. Sometimes, Mendel let this generation self reproduce which produced a **Second Filial Generation (F2)**.

With this experiments, Mendel rejected blending theory and gave support to **particulate inheritance**.

He found that In **F1** just one trait appeared, while in **F2** both traits appeared. The trait that appeared the most is the **dominant** and the other the **recessive**.

### Correlation Between Medel's Results and Today's Knowledge
Every character is a **gene** and while both gametes are **haploids**, the resulting zygote is **diploid**. Furthermore, depending of the form of the gene, **the allele**, is the trait that is presented.

Now, an organism can be either **homozygous** (equal alleles) or **heterozygous** (different alleles). If the organism is homozygous, it will have the traits of that gene, conversely if the organism is heterozygous it will have the genes of the **dominant allele**.

### Mendel's Laws
1. **Law of Segregation:** The gamete will only have one of the two copies of a gene.
2. **Law of Independent Assortment:** Alleles of different genes assort independently of one another during gamete formation. ( Just true when genes are distant from one another in the chromosome).

**Gene:** A short section of DNA.
Generally **dominant genes** are expressed, while **recessive genes** are mutated and not expressed.

### Probability to Predict Inheritance
The probability of two outcomes occurring together is found by **multiplying** the individual probabilities.

### Generalities of Alleles

- Alleles are not always **recessive**.
- **One allele** can have more than one **phenotypic effect**.
